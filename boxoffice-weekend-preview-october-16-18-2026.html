Jailer 2: the biggest Tamil pre-sale of the year, and what it is not

The line that matters is Hindi. It has 4,504 shows - more than Telugu's 3,237 - and takes Rs 3.9k a show against Tamil's Rs 33.4k. About a ninth, on a comparable number of screens.

Our own sweep found out why

We ran District - the ticketing app we sweep ourselves - across 133 cities on Wednesday evening: 5,429 shows, 671 cinemas, 692,834 seats sold, 44.9% occupancy, Rs 16.68 crore. Of the 1,004 Hindi shows on that read, exactly 2 were in PVR, INOX or Cinepolis. That is 0.2%. The Hindi version has been given a wide release and almost no national multiplex chain.

Worldwide

Day-one pre-sales passed Rs 60 crore gross worldwide on Wednesday morning, split roughly Rs 31 crore India and $3.1M overseas - about Rs 30 crore. Overseas was therefore running level with the home market, which is unusual even for a Rajinikanth film.

Our opening-day call: Rs 60 crore India net

Rs 60 Cr — band Rs 54-67 crore, India net, all languages. Unchanged from Wednesday night.

We moved this call twice and it ended where it started, which deserves an explanation rather than a victory lap. On Wednesday evening we said Rs 55 crore. We revised to Rs 60 on two arguments: that our advance figure was a midnight snapshot being compared against Coolie's final, and that Jailer 2's advance was far thinner per show than Coolie's, leaving more to sell at the counter.

The first argument was wrong. We predicted the advance would add 10-15% overnight to reach Rs 44-46 crore. It added 2.1%, from Rs 40.10 crore to Rs 41.48 crore. There was no morning surge.

The second was right, and it matters more. Strip the block seats from both films and Jailer 2 has sold more real advance tickets than Coolie did - 17.94 lakh against 17.30 lakh - for 1.4% less money. That gap is the Tamil Nadu ticket price cap and nothing else. Coolie opened to Rs 65 crore net.

The two errors roughly cancel, so Rs 60 crore stands. Count people, not rupees: a Tamil Nadu ticket is capped, so the rupee figure understates how many bodies this film is moving.

Counter-programming

Five new releases in 48 hours, and only one of them is counter-programming

Friday brings three wide releases plus a fourth, smaller Telugu title, and a fifth follows on Saturday. Only one of them is really aimed at a different crowd; the rest are chasing the same ticket Jailer 2 already has. And note the calendar: Dussehra itself is Tuesday 20 October, so this is the weekend before the holiday, not the holiday weekend. Everything here is positioned to ride into it. On paper that is a crowded run-up. In the advance data it is not a contest.

Ranabaali: the headline is 87% block seats

Read this before quoting its advance

Ranabaali's day-one advance reads Rs 86 lakh. Excluding block seats it is Rs 11 lakh. That is 87% of the headline bought in bulk - 67,257 tickets become 8,235. Occupancy goes from 22% to 3%.

For scale, Jailer 2's block seats are 9.4% of its headline. Both figures are real money to the exhibitor. Neither is evidence of public demand, and the difference between 9% and 87% is the whole story.

Telugu releases do carry a low advance culture - Jailer (2023) saw only 17.8% of its Telugu day one pre-booked against 36.6% in Tamil - so a thin advance on its own is not damning. But Rs 11 lakh of genuine public pre-sale on 709 shows, for a Vijay Deverakonda and Rashmika Mandanna period epic across five languages, is thinner than that habit alone explains. Its average ticket of Rs 134 is also the lowest on the board.

Prahaar and Nayi Naveli: the same shape, 24 hours apart

Both are Hindi, both built for multiplexes, both with essentially no advance and not one sold-out show between them across 4,420 shows. Nayi Naveli is the wider and cheaper of the two - 2,530 shows at Rs 251 a ticket against 1,890 at Rs 298 - which is what a PVR Inox distribution buys you.

The reversal is worth noting. On the raw numbers Nayi Naveli looks ahead at 2.97% sold to Prahaar's 2.62%. Cleaned of the bad-venue data, it is behind: 1.98% against 2.29%. It wins on footprint, not on demand.

And the one that stings for Prahaar: it is a film about a Mumbai public prosecutor, released with no Marathi version at all, and Maharashtra - its largest state footprint at 71 cinemas - is at 2.6%. Pune, which ought to be the most receptive city in the country for this subject, sits at 1.7% on 106 shows.

A trap other trackers will fall into today

Five single-screen venues sold 34% of every Nayi Naveli ticket in India, on 19 of 2,530 shows, at 63-83% occupancy while the country sits at 2%. Two of them reported exactly the same numbers, seat for seat, for Prahaar - New Kohinoor in Jodhpur at 744 of 1,140 seats for both films, APR in Gaya at 83.5% for both and 85.8% for Jailer 2. Two different films cannot sell the same seats. That is a stale default the app never updated, not real demand. It puts four fake cities at the top of the hot list, and anyone sorting the city column today will publish them.

The incumbent

Drishyam: The Conclusion finally gets tested

It has had an extraordinarily soft run. Released 2 October, it crossed Rs 500 crore worldwide on day 12 - the first Indian film to do so since Dhurandhar: The Revenge in March, 205 days earlier - and it has not faced a single wide release aimed at its audience.

On Friday it is day 15, and it gives up screens to five films at once.

What it takes to reach Rs 200 crore

Rs 200 crore across four days needs opening day at Rs 64 crore or better - the top half of our Rs 54-67 band. On our central Rs 60 crore it lands at Rs 188 crore, which is close but not there. Everything in that column moves with Thursday and almost nothing else, so the day-one figure settling tonight decides the headline for the whole run.

And the run does not stop on Sunday

Dussehra falls on Tuesday 20 October - day six of Jailer 2's run, not this weekend. Every film here is positioned ahead of the holiday rather than on it, which means the Monday dip should be shallower than normal and Tuesday gets a second opening-weekend-sized day. A six-day figure to Dussehra night is the number the trade will end up quoting, and on our central call - Monday near Rs 19 crore, a Dussehra Tuesday near Rs 28 crore - it lands around Rs 235 crore.

The one nobody is watching

Hanuman Ansh is still running, and it may yet pass Drishyam

Day 70 on Friday. Rs 337.08 crore India net through day 67 - Tuesday 13 October, the last day our daily chart has settled - on a reported Rs 2 crore budget, with Rs 437.88 crore worldwide gross. It has been taking over a crore a day for weeks.

Something strange happened in week 10. On the headline it rose 19.5% on week 9's daily average, which at day 70 is close to unheard of. But split it by language and the Hindi original is running Rs 0.87 crore a day - down 42.5% on its own week 9, a perfectly ordinary fall. The entire rise is a Telugu version that released on 9 October - day 63, the last day of week 9 - so week 10 is the first full week it has been in cinemas. The dub is already out-earning the original.

That is an outside chance rather than a likely one, and it would need two things at once: Drishyam's third weekend collapsing under this slate, and the Telugu dub holding into November. But it is a live enough possibility to be worth saying out loud, and the crossover condition is simple enough to watch week by week.

What we are not claiming

We withdrew the "157 times its budget" line as unverifiable and are not reintroducing a bigger version of it. The Rs 2 crore budget is a reported figure, not a filing. There is still no streaming deal; the producer has denied the Rs 100 crore figure on the record. And on worldwide, the Rs 500 crore club is out of reach - overseas is only 12.4% of its total and would have to grow by half again from here.

North American ticket sales are reported to open today at 9:00 AM ET. We swept every US platform we can read last night and found zero bookable showtimes - which, twelve hours early, is the correct answer rather than a worrying one.

Four US ticketing sites, four different release dates, on the morning sales are supposed to open. Sony's own posters say 6 November, the producer has said 8 November for India, and the trade has reported 5 November as official. It is not settled.

Two findings from that sweep are worth carrying. The English cut has no listing anywhere - a 2h45m English version was reported weeks ago, and not one US platform we can read carries it; only the Hindi 3h15m cut is bookable. And Canada has nothing at all, not one chain.

On the trailer: the first landed 30 July and is reported to have crossed 100 crore views in five days. A second was announced as "coming soon" on 6 October and neither Prime Focus nor Namit Malhotra has put a date on it since. If it lands this weekend it lands into a Jailer 2 news cycle, which is a choice someone has had to make.

The competitive fact that matters most

Godzilla Minus Zero opens 6 November in North America with IMAX early access from 3 November, and its tickets are already on sale. It is also a "Filmed For IMAX" title. On the exact premium big-screen showtimes Ramayana's North American pitch depends on, the rival is in the building first and selling. Ramayana has no IMAX showtime listed anywhere.

How to read this

What is measured, what is modelled, and what we will grade

Advance figures are gross and come from Sacnilk unless named otherwise. Collections are India net. Our own sweeps are named as ours. Every projection in this piece is modelled and labelled, and the weekend chart is an estimate in full - not a tracker read.

How much did Jailer 2 earn in advance booking?

What is Jailer 2's opening day prediction?
