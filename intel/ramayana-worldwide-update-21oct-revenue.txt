WHERE THE "BEFORE" COLUMN COMES FROM
  Everything is the page as it stood on 20 Oct except the US dollar figure,
  which has to be derived: the strip published $876,004 for North America,
  Cineplex was $39,398 and Landmark $8,761, so the US was $827,845. That
  checks independently against the App.jsx comment recording a blended ATP
  of $18.94 on 43,708 tickets - 43,708 x 18.94 = $827,830, fifteen dollars
  off the derived figure, which is the ATP rounding to two places and not a
  discrepancy. Worldwide before = 108,518 tickets / $1,440,831, which is
  what we published yesterday.

FIVE THINGS TO CHECK BEFORE POSTING

1. THE REVENUE PERCENTAGES ARE TICKET PERCENTAGES. Four of the five
   markets that moved have a FIXED assumed price, so their revenue % and
   ticket % are identical to the decimal. Only two differ at all: the US
   by 0.3pp because its format mix shifted the blend $18.94 -> $18.98, and
   Germany by 0.7pp because its cinemas' own printed prices went
   EUR 17.11 -> 17.21. Nobody should read these as pricing power.

2. THE +9.8% vs +7.2% GAP IS MIX, AND IT IS WORTH EXPLAINING RATHER THAN
   CLAIMING. India is 40% of worldwide tickets at $6.67 and did not move
   today. Every market that did move sells at $17-19. So the world average
   ticket rose 2.5% without one price changing. Post 3 exists because
   "revenue up 9.8%, tickets up 7.2%" invites exactly the wrong inference.

3. THE THREE ZEROES ARE NOT FLAT DEMAND. India, Australia & NZ and the
   Netherlands read 0% because they were not re-read today, not because
   nobody bought a ticket. India in particular is one date on one platform.
   Post 2 says so on the same line; do not cut it for characters.

4. LANDMARK'S +95.7% IS 86% COVERAGE. 554 of the 647 is 36 ScreenX
   showtimes nobody had ever opened. It is the biggest percentage on the
   board and the least meaningful one, which is why it gets its own post
   instead of sitting in the table unqualified.

5. $1,582,627 IS A VALUATION, NOT BOX OFFICE. Germany is the only market
   where the cinemas print a price, and only for 49% of its tickets.
   India's rupees are read off District. Everything else applies our own
   assumed ATP. No money has changed hands in any of these figures.
