Where Bhooth Bangla Stands in Akshay Kumar's Filmography

At ~₹244 Cr worldwide (BCM-reconciled), Bhooth Bangla is now the 8th highest-grossing film of Akshay Kumar's career. It has already surpassed his other major hits — Airlift (₹228 Cr, 2016), OMG 2 (₹221 Cr, 2023), Rustom (₹216 Cr, 2016) and Kesari (₹209 Cr, 2019) — and still has runway through Week 5. Ahead of it sit Akshay's career peaks: 2.0 (₹691 Cr, Tamil-led), Toilet (₹317 Cr), Good Newwz (₹316 Cr), Housefull 4 (₹296 Cr), Sooryavanshi (₹293 Cr), Mission Mangal (₹290 Cr), and Housefull 5 (₹289 Cr). Signal 1 — The non-holiday April opener. Look at the release-type column above. Of the seven Akshay films ranked above Bhooth Bangla, every single one had either a holiday window (Diwali, Independence Day, Christmas/NY) or a Tamil-original release (2.0). Bhooth Bangla is the highest-grossing non-holiday opener of Akshay's career by a meaningful margin. The opening weekend ₹58 Cr came on a normal April weekend without a national festival window propping up footfalls — a stronger commercial signal than the ranking number alone suggests.

Signal 2 — The post-pandemic reset. Akshay's 2021–2024 run included multiple underperformers (Bachchhan Paandey, Samrat Prithviraj, Raksha Bandhan, Selfiee, Cuttputlli). 2025 brought three decent grossers but no HIT verdicts. Bhooth Bangla is his first clear HIT in over four years by trade definition — Pinkvilla, Koimoi, and Glamsham all explicitly use that framing. The 14-year Priyadarshan reunion worked exactly as intended: restoring Akshay's comedy brand without leaning on franchise IP or a tentpole slot.

Signal 3 — The 2026 industry ranking. Per Wikipedia's 2026 tally, Bhooth Bangla is the 4th highest-grossing Indian film of 2026 and the 3rd highest-grossing Hindi film of 2026 — behind only Dhurandhar 2 and Border 2. Per Koimoi, it's also now the 4th highest-grossing Indian horror-comedy ever globally, ahead of Bhool Bhulaiyaa 2. That's serious company for a horror-comedy with no IP backing and a mid-tier ₹120 Cr budget.

Where Our BCM Got It Wrong

Our April 23 BCM closed Week 1 at ₹89 Cr (called within 1%) and projected a lifetime base case of ₹138 Cr with a ₹145 Cr ceiling. The film passed ₹138 Cr on Day 18 (BCM-reconciled) and the ceiling of ₹145 Cr around Day 20. Two weeks later it has cleared both by ₹11 Cr+ and is still adding.

What the model missed: we calibrated against Sitaare Zameen Par (λ=0.57) and Housefull 5 (λ=0.82), landing at λ=0.74. The actual decay turned out closer to λ=0.64 — the film held W2 at ~46% of W1 (we'd projected ~33%), and W3 retained ~48% of W2. That kind of family-comedy repeat-viewing pattern in weeks 2–3 was outside the comparable model's expected range. Priyadarshan's 14-year reunion narrative kept driving families back for second viewings well past the typical comedy-genre fall-off curve.

BCM v1.2 calibration: adding a "Priyadarshan + Akshay co-occurrence factor" to the decay model. Historical pairings (Hera Pheri, De Dana Dan, Garam Masala, Bhagam Bhag) all showed unusually flat W2–W3 curves. The pattern was real; our model didn't see it because we only used 2025 comparables. Lesson logged for the next horror-comedy or Priyadarshan release.
