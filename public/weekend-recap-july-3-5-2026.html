01Alpha: the film that beat its own smear campaign

Let's start with the film that walked in with a target on its back. For weeks before release, Alpha — Alia Bhatt as Sita, Sharvari as Durga, Bobby Deol as the villain, a Hrithik cameo — was buried under a coordinated wave of online negativity and open exhibitor scepticism. A female-led spy film with no male superstar headlining it? The trade had all but written the obituary. Yash Raj answered the only way that matters: with a release size nobody saw coming, and a film that grew every single day of its opening weekend. ₹9.25 Cr Friday, ₹11.50 Cr Saturday, ₹13.25 Cr Sunday — occupancy climbing from 20% to 29% — for a three-day total of ₹34 crore nett (₹40.88 Cr gross). Bollywood Hungama said the scale of the release "caught the trade by surprise." For the first woman-fronted entry in the Spy Universe, opening double-digits and building through the weekend against that much noise isn't a soft landing — it's a film beating the story that had been written about it.

Is it flawless? No, and we won't pretend otherwise. Monday cooled to ₹3.85 Cr, and against a reported ₹100 crore-plus budget the weekday hold is the test that decides everything now. But a weekend that climbs instead of fading is the single cleanest sign that audiences liked what they saw more than the trolls wanted them to. The doom scenario was a disaster open; Alpha delivered the opposite. The job now is turning that defiance into a run.

Grading our own homework — honestly

Before release we published a BCM call for Alpha's North America weekend: $1.3M–$2.6M, central ~$1.8M. It did $574K (overseas total $1.65M) — we missed, on the high side. And here's the honest reconciliation, because it cuts both ways: the pre-release crowd got the India opening wrong on the low side, and we got the overseas number wrong on the high side. We over-weighted the Spy Universe brand and the Hrithik cameo; abroad, the leads' thinner solo track record was the stronger signal. Box office humbles everyone — trolls and analysts alike. India is where Alpha answered its critics; overseas is where it, and our forecast, came up short. We don't hide either.

02Welcome to the Jungle: the film that won the weekend without opening

Here's the twist the trade didn't see coming: the biggest Hindi story of the weekend was a film in its second week. Welcome to the Jungle should have wilted when Alpha grabbed its screens — it lost nearly 2,400 shows on Friday. Instead it grew. A ₹4.50 Cr second Friday climbed to ₹7.35 Cr Saturday and ₹9.75 Cr Sunday — a genuine weekend build in week two, which is the single cleanest signal that audiences actually like a film. Total India nett is now around ₹115 crore, and worldwide it's crossed ₹169.76 crore, overtaking Cocktail 2 to become Bollywood's 4th-biggest global grosser of 2026.

The engine is exactly what you'd expect from a Welcome film: family audiences and the mass heartland circuits, nostalgia plus Akshay's timing, powered by word of mouth rather than hype. Overseas has quietly become a highlight too, out-pacing several of Akshay's post-COVID releases at the same stage. Akshay is heading for his second ₹200 Cr grosser of the year. The only cloud on the horizon is Dhamaal 4 arriving soon to contest the family audience — but for now, the man who opened two weeks ago is beating the woman who opened on Friday.

The one that refuses to end

Main Vaapas Aaunga is still climbing in WEEK FOUR

I wrote about this film a month ago as the sleeper of June, and I half-expected it to be gone by now. It isn't. Imtiaz Ali's Main Vaapas Aaunga — Diljit Dosanjh, an A.R. Rahman score — is in its fourth weekend and still adding, its fourth Saturday pulling ₹1.75 Cr and the total nearing ₹55 crore. Films that open big are supposed to obey the decay curve. This one never read the manual.

04US box office, July 4 weekend 2026: Minions & Monsters tops a quiet Fourth

US box office in one line: Over the July 3–5, 2026 Independence Day weekend, Minions & Monsters opened #1 with a franchise-low $36.4M (3-day), Toy Story 5 held second with $31M, and Angel Studios' Young Washington surprised at #3 with $20.8M — a healthy but unspectacular holiday frame, with total ticket sales running below the usual July 4 pace.

The US spent its 250th birthday at the movies, and honestly? The box office is in good shape — it just didn't throw a party for its new number one. Minions & Monsters, the seventh Despicable Me film, took the top spot with $36.4M over three days ($61.4M since its Wednesday launch) — and here's the irony: it landed the best reviews the franchise has ever had, a 91% on Rotten Tomatoes. A good movie, a muted opening. Blame Toy Story 5 fatigue, blame families saving their money for Moana next week, blame July 4th falling on a Saturday. Seven films in sixteen years will also do it — even the little yellow guys can get overexposed.

06FAQ

How much did Alpha earn in its opening weekend?

Alpha collected ₹34 crore nett (₹40.88 Cr gross) in India over its July 3–5 opening weekend, growing each day (₹9.25 / ₹11.50 / ₹13.25 Cr). Overseas it managed $1.65M, with North America at $574K. Worldwide 3-day gross was about ₹55 crore. It then dropped ~71% on Monday, a concerning weekday trend against its reported ₹100 crore-plus budget.

Did Boxoffy's Alpha North America prediction come true?

No — and we own it. We predicted an NA opening weekend of $1.3M–$2.6M (central ~$1.8M). Alpha actually did $574K in North America, well below even our floor. We over-weighted the YRF Spy Universe brand and the Hrithik cameo, and under-weighted the weak solo overseas track records of both leads — which turned out to be the stronger signal.

How is Welcome to the Jungle holding in week 2?

Very well. Its second weekend grew across the days (₹4.50 / ₹7.35 / ₹9.75 Cr), taking India nett to about ₹115 crore and worldwide past ₹169.76 crore — Bollywood's 4th-biggest global grosser of 2026. Strong word of mouth and family/mass-circuit demand are driving it, even after losing screens to Alpha.

Is Main Vaapas Aaunga still running?

Yes — remarkably, it's in its fourth weekend and still adding, nearing ₹55 crore nett on word of mouth alone, against a ~₹60 crore budget. It's the standout legs story of the summer.

What was number one at the US box office on July 4 weekend?

Minions & Monsters debuted at #1 with $36.4M (a soft opening despite franchise-best 91% reviews). Obsession continued its historic run past $403M worldwide in week 8, and Spielberg's Disclosure Day crossed $100M domestic.

How did Minions & Monsters perform at the box office?

Minions & Monsters opened to $36.4M over the July 3–5 three-day weekend and $61.4M since its Wednesday launch — the lowest start in the Despicable Me/Minions franchise. Despite the soft debut it earned the franchise's best reviews (91% on Rotten Tomatoes) and reached about $161M worldwide in its first days, so it remains a hit against its $85M budget.

How much has Obsession made at the box office?

Obsession has crossed $403M worldwide (over $245M domestic) as of early July 2026 — an extraordinary result for a film made for under $1 million, making it the highest-grossing film ever with a sub-$1M budget. It was still adding $5.3M in its eighth weekend.
