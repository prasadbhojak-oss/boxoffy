Three Trailers, One Hype Index — Cocktail 2, Welcome to the Jungle & Alpha

Three of the year's biggest Hindi titles dropped trailers in ten days. Here's BCM's read on each — trailer reception, social-media sentiment and trade tracking — with opening-day and opening-weekend calls, in order of release.

Boxoffy Editorial Desk·Thursday, June 11, 2026·Hype Index
The three contenders

In a single ten-day stretch, three of 2026's most-anticipated Hindi films laid their cards on the table: a romantic-drama sequel, a 30-star comedy, and a spy-universe origin story. Trailers don't open films — Day-1 footfalls and word-of-mouth do — but they're the first hard signal of where heat is gathering. Below, BCM scores each on the Hype Index and maps it to an opening-day and opening-weekend range. Every call here is pre-release and trailer-stage, with no advance booking yet, so treat them as warm reads, not verdicts — we'll grade them when each film lands.

1Cocktail 2

In cinemas · June 19, 2026 · trailer out June 2

Fourteen years after the original became a romantic touchstone, Homi Adajania returns with a standalone sequel — Shahid Kapoor as Kunal, Kriti Sanon as Ally and Rashmika Mandanna as Diya, with Dimple Kapadia and Rohit Saraf, Pritam back on music and Maddock Films behind it. The trailer trades the original's story for a fresh love-and-friendship triangle set against glossy Italian backdrops.

The read: the trailer sparked an immediate social-media frenzy on June 2, and the standout talking point isn't the romance — it's the charged Kriti–Rashmika dynamic, which several reactions found more compelling than either woman's equation with Shahid. Pritam's "Mashooka" is already a crowd favourite, and the glossy Sicily visuals read as money well spent. The risk is genre: a pure romantic drama is a tougher theatrical sell than it once was, so this one leans heavily on music and word-of-mouth. Rashmika's pan-India pull (post-Pushpa, Animal) is the X-factor that could widen the base beyond the usual urban-romance audience.

2Welcome to the Jungle

In cinemas · June 26, 2026 · trailer out June 11

The Welcome franchise goes bigger and broader — a 30-plus-star ensemble led by Akshay Kumar, with the Hera Pheri trio of Akshay, Suniel Shetty and Paresh Rawal reunited, plus Jackie Shroff, Sanjay Dutt, Disha Patani, Jacqueline Fernandez, Arshad Warsi and a long bench more. Directed by Ahmed Khan for Firoz Nadiadwala, the trailer launched at a reported ₹1.5-crore YRF Studios event and sells pure jungle-set comic chaos around a "₹2,000-crore fake film."

The read: the single biggest draw is nostalgia — the Akshay–Suniel–Paresh camaraderie is the most-shared beat of the trailer, tapping straight into Hera Pheri affection. A 30-star marquee generates its own mass curiosity for a family-comedy summer release. But the reaction isn't uniformly warm: a vocal slice of trade and audiences read it as loud and recycled ("30 stars, one jungle, zero original ideas"), and Ahmed Khan's recent comedy track record tempers expectations. This is a volume play — broad, festive, single-screen-friendly — and its ceiling depends on whether the comedy lands or just looks busy.

3Alpha

In cinemas · July 2026 · teaser out ~June 10

The seventh entry in the YRF Spy Universe — and its first female-led one. Alia Bhatt plays Sita, an agent raised from childhood to kill, opposite Sharvari, with Bobby Deol and Anil Kapoor in pivotal roles, directed by Shiv Rawail (The Railway Men) for Aditya Chopra. The teaser frames it as an edgy, VFX-heavy origin story slotting into the Pathaan–Tiger–War continuity.

The read: this carries the highest pedigree of the three — the Spy Universe is Hindi cinema's most bankable brand, and the prospect of Alia and Sharvari fronting it is generating real anticipation. Two caveats keep our confidence lowest here. First, it's a teaser, not a full trailer — there's less to judge, and the score is built more on franchise equity than footage. Second, the date has slipped repeatedly (December 2025 → April → July), and a female-led spy entry is commercially untested against the ₹50-crore-plus openings the male-fronted universe films posted. The pedigree says big; the unknowns say wide bands.

The Dhurandhar problem: the teaser also walked into a buzzsaw on X — heavy trolling and a flood of unfavourable comparisons to Dhurandhar, the Ranveer Singh franchise that crossed ₹3,000 crore across two films and reset what audiences expect from spy-action. That comparison is, strictly, the wrong yardstick: Dhurandhar is gritty, grounded realism; Alpha is a deliberately glossy, larger-than-life universe entertainer — different sub-genres, different intent, and even part of X said as much. But the expectation gap it created is real. Fair or not, Alpha now has to clear a bar it didn't set, and the cool teaser reception is a genuine signal — which is why we've marked the Hype Index down. The mitigants are equally real: this is only a teaser, A-listers from Shah Rukh Khan to Amitabh Bachchan publicly backed it, and a strong full trailer plus music can reset the mood quickly. We count the sentiment, not the comparison.

BCM read · the throughlineThree different bets on the same summer. Cocktail 2 is the music-and-word-of-mouth play, with the Kriti–Rashmika dynamic as its breakout hook. Welcome to the Jungle is the volume play — nostalgia and a 30-star marquee against a "seen-this-before" risk. Alpha is the pedigree play, carrying the strongest brand but the least to actually judge — and its teaser just cooled the mood, with X benchmarking it against Dhurandhar (the wrong yardstick, but a real expectation gap), which is why it slips to the back of this pack on hype. The Hype scores cluster tight (62–67) precisely because each leads on a different signal — and none has the Day-1 anchor that would let BCM tighten the bands. That comes with advance bookings, two to four days out from each release.
