What we got wrong. We came in light on the US opening, and the reason is worth spelling out. We treated IMAX as a ceiling on how many people could see the film, when it was really raising what each of them paid. The Oppenheimer comparison is the one worth sitting with. Back in 2023, that film's 70mm run took $17M from 30 screens over four weeks. The Odyssey took $6.3M from 41 screens in three days. Premium formats carried 53% of the domestic gross, with 70mm seats going at $22.50 against $19 for the rest of the PLF slate. Tickets went on sale a full year ahead and vanished in minutes, which is how venues ended up running 2am, 3am and 6am shows. Even IMAX's Monday held at a fifth of its weekend, where 10% is the norm.

Dhamaal 4 deserves more credit than it's getting. It closed week one at ₹96 Cr and crossed ₹100 Cr on day eight, a first for the franchise, and it did that while dropping only 8.3% on the Friday The Odyssey was eating every premium screen in the country. Comedies in their second week usually break when something this size lands on top of them. Instead the two ran side by side, and neither seems to have taken much from the other.

Why 71 and not higher. The demand is real, but it stops at the state border. Tamil Nadu is treating this like an occasion; nowhere else is. Leo opened to ₹148.5 Cr worldwide and GOAT to ₹126 Cr, and this isn't in that company. The leak is the other reason we've kept the ceiling where it is. What the film does have is finality, and that tends not to fade over three days the way ordinary star hype does. Saturday will settle it. A Vijay film that climbs into its first Saturday behaves very differently from one that takes everything on day one and drifts.

Independence Day, 2026: the rematch.

On 29 June 2007, Sunny Deol's Apne opened against Emraan Hashmi's Awarapan. Sunny won it comfortably: ₹69.28 Cr against ₹12 Cr, on budgets of ₹20 Cr and ₹18 Cr respectively. Awarapan lost money, then spent the next two decades becoming a cult record on the strength of Pritam's soundtrack. Nineteen years on, both men are back on the same Friday — 14 August 2026, with Independence Day landing on the Saturday.

Sunny Deol and Preity Zinta, directed by Rajkumar Santoshi, produced by Aamir Khan Productions. A partition drama — previously titled Lahore 1947 — landing on Independence Day weekend. That is the tightest subject-to-date alignment on the 2026 calendar, and Sunny's post-Gadar 2 pull with the single-screen and North belt is the upside case.
Awarapan 2 · Hype 61
₹5–8 Cr
BCM Day 1 net · early read
Emraan Hashmi returns as Shivam Pandit, 19 years on, with Disha Patani. Directed by Nitin Kakkar for Vishesh Films, and written as a direct continuation rather than a reboot. Vishesh Films is betting that two decades of accumulated affection converts into opening-weekend footfall, which is a harder trick than it sounds: the people who made the first film a cult object mostly did so at home, years after it left cinemas.

These two aren't really fighting each other. A partition drama and a neo-noir revenge romance are not chasing the same ticket, and both can work on a holiday weekend. What they are fighting over is screens. With two Hindi releases on a three-day frame, exhibitors settle the allocation on Thursday night, and that decides a good deal of both openings before anyone buys a seat. A subject that matches the date usually wins that room, which is where Batwara starts ahead. Worth noting too: Shabana Azmi is in both films. Treat these ranges as a first read — advance booking opens around 11 August, and the numbers get sharp then.
