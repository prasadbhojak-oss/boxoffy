The India read. ₹180.20 Cr net (₹215.47 Cr gross) across three days is the biggest Hollywood run India has ever seen, comfortably past Endgame’s ₹158.65 Cr and Avatar 2’s ₹155 Cr three-day cumulatives. Day 3 came in at ₹70.25 Cr, a 42.4% jump over Day 2 — which is the elite Saturday hold pattern that only the very biggest films produce. Language split Day 3: English ₹38.35 Cr (69.67% occupancy in 3D shows), Hindi ₹21.75 Cr, Tamil ₹3.75 Cr, Telugu ₹2.75 Cr. Sacnilk is now tracking a ₹300 Cr four-day India close.

The BookMyShow signal. Brand New Day has already crossed 4 million BookMyShow tickets across the opening window and is on track to become the first Hollywood film to sell 1 million tickets in a single day on BMS since August 2023. Sunday’s hourly peak hit 63,410 tickets sold between 7–8 PM.

“Audiences are tired of seeing the same thing. Too many connected storylines. It’s turning entertainment into homework. This confirms there is truly no such thing as Spider-Man fatigue.” Jeff Bock, Exhibitor Relations · via Variety

China: the surprise that unlocked the ceiling. BND opened Wednesday to $37M Day 1 and closed the 3-day at $119M, the biggest superhero opening in China in seven years. China IMAX alone did $19M, the biggest Spider-Man IMAX opening in the market’s history. Marvel’s day-and-date China strategy — abandoned after No Way Home skipped the market entirely in 2021 — is back on the table for every future MCU release.

The Odyssey angle. Nolan’s film crossed ₹172 Cr through 16 days in India, cementing its place as his biggest ever release here. Together, BND and Odyssey have delivered the biggest Hollywood weekend Indian cinema has ever seen, and it is happening while three domestic films continue to hold the top-5 alongside them.

What actually broke this weekend:

  • All-time US opening day record (Endgame $157M → BND $168M)
  • All-time US Thursday previews record (Endgame $60M → BND $72M)
  • Biggest Hollywood opening in Indian box-office history (Endgame ₹53.60 Cr → BND ₹60.60 Cr Day 1, ₹158.65 Cr → ₹180.20 Cr 3-day)
  • Biggest China Spider-Man IMAX opening ever ($19M)
  • Highest-grossing Hollywood weekend in Indian history (BND + Odyssey combined)

What did not break — yet. Endgame’s $1.22B global weekend, Endgame’s $357M US weekend (by $2M, pending Monday actuals), and Endgame’s ₹373 Cr India lifetime net. On our BCM projection, the India lifetime record breaks in the next 10–14 days.

This is a Spider-Man weekend, and it isn't close.

Brand New Day swung into Indian cinemas on Thursday and immediately rewrote the Hollywood record book, tracking to the biggest opening day a non-Indian film has ever had here. Everything else this weekend is playing for second place. The good news for the holdovers is that Spider-Man's audience and theirs barely overlap. Nolan's Odyssey has its premium-format loyalists, Jana Nayagan has Vijay's Tamil base, and the family and South-language films keep ticking along underneath the web-slinger's shadow. The holdovers are holding well: Odyssey is still pulling ₹18–24 Cr in its third weekend on IMAX legs, Jana Nayagan crossed ₹175 Cr net and stays the South's anchor, and Dhamaal 4 has quietly banked ₹155 Cr net across four weeks. None of them are being killed by Spider-Man; they're just sharing a very crowded marquee.

Spider-Man: the records in reach.

93/100
The biggest Hollywood opening India has ever seen Record presales, a five-language release, and premium-format demand that had Hindi shows at 100% morning occupancy. On Day 1 it's tracking past the ₹53.10 Cr net that Avengers: Endgame has held since 2019.

We won't over-analyse a film this obviously huge. The story is simply which records fall. Here's the ladder, all of them held by Avengers: Endgame, the benchmark for Hollywood in India:

The BookMyShow signal is electric
  1. First Hollywood film ever to sell 50,000 tickets in a single hour on BMS — no international release has matched that hourly velocity since the feature launched in 2023.
  2. 2 million+ tickets in pre-sales, roughly 4× Deadpool & Wolverine's 505K, the biggest Hollywood pre-seller on the platform since Aug 2023.
  3. ~6.15 lakh tickets on July 29 alone, a late surge on top of the ~1.39M sold across the prior six weeks.
  4. 5.80 lakh national-chain tickets (PVR/INOX/Cinepolis) for Day 1, the second-biggest Hollywood advance in Indian history behind only Endgame.

The five records Spider-Man is chasing in India
  1. Biggest opening day — Endgame's ₹53.10 Cr net (2019). Brand New Day is tracking ₹55–60 Cr on Day 1, and looks set to take it.
  2. Biggest opening weekend — Endgame's ₹157 Cr net over three days. A ₹55–plus Thursday puts this within range by Sunday.
  3. Biggest opening week — beating Endgame's ₹315 Cr gross first week.
  4. Highest-grossing Hollywood film ever in India — past Avatar: The Way of Water's ₹477.50 Cr gross lifetime.
  5. First ₹500 Cr Hollywood film in India — a club no non-Indian film has ever entered.

The first two are near-certain to fall this weekend. The last two are lifetime targets that depend on how the film holds through August, but the fact that they're even on the table tells you the scale of what's happening. Globally, the picture rhymes: our BCM reads a ~$135M US opening day and a ~$285–295M weekend, with a worldwide bow near $640M, second only to Endgame's all-time $1.223B. China, which No Way Home never had, opened at Endgame-era pace.

Our honest score. The Spider-Man call was solid but too cautious: our ₹140–165 Cr projection assumed a normal Marvel opening trajectory; actuals came in at ₹197.60 Cr because BND played like an event, not a release. Jana Nayagan’s week-2 hold rewarded us on the upside. The five misses on smaller titles all reflect the same dynamic — Spider-Man’s show count cannibalised morning and afternoon slots across regional-language sites. Odyssey’s week-3 miss is the same story: IMAX and premium screens shifted to BND on Thursday and stayed there. Our BCM lesson from this weekend: when one film consumes 60%+ of premium capacity, everyone else’s upside range compresses.

Off the big screen: the OTT week.

While Spider-Man owns the theatres, streaming has its own busy weekend as August's slate opens up. Here's the broad sweep of what's moving to digital and what's new across the major platforms.

SonyLIVWelcome to the Jungle, Akshay Kumar's ensemble comedy, makes its digital debut after its theatrical run — one of August's biggest post-theatrical drops. Maharani S2 (Huma Qureshi) also lands later in the month.
Apple TV+Ted Lasso S3 returns after three years, with Jason Sudeikis now coaching a second-division women's team, one new episode every Wednesday.
Prime VideoReacher and Outer Banks headline the international slate, alongside post-theatrical Tamil and Telugu titles including Love Oh Love in four South languages.
NetflixLeads the volume as usual with a wave of post-theatrical films and series, plus WWE continuing its live-in-India streaming across all formats.
JioHotstarOperation Safed Sagar, the Kargil air-war drama, is among the war and action titles arriving digitally this month.
ZEE5Continues its regional-heavy slate; Rangbaaz S4, Bharat Bhagya Vidhaata and Bandar are reported for digital soon, dates still to be locked.

The pattern this month is theatrical-to-digital catch-up, with July's cinema hits starting their streaming lives, layered under returning international tentpoles. For the films still in cinemas on our Top 10, an OTT date is the next milestone to watch; none of this weekend's theatrical leaders has a digital window announced yet.

On the horizon: Ramayana and Batwara.

RamayanaRamayanaDiwali · Nov 8

Two very different films are shaping the road ahead. Ramayana — the ₹4,000 Cr, two-part epic with Ranbir Kapoor, Yash and Sai Pallavi — counts down to a Diwali release on Nov 8, now carrying Sony Pictures for international distribution and the tag of the first Indian film to be “Filmed for IMAX.” Our early read has it opening in the ₹175–215 Cr net range in India. If Spider-Man is this weekend's event, Ramayana is the year's.

Sooner than that, the Batwara 1947 trailer is landing ahead of its Aug 14 release. It stars Sunny Deol and Preity Zinta in a Rajkumar Santoshi partition drama, produced by Aamir Khan Productions with an A.R. Rahman score. It opens Independence Day weekend against Emraan Hashmi's Awarapan 2, and the trailer is the first real look at whether Santoshi has another Gadar-sized swing in him. We'll have the full Hype Index read once it's out.
