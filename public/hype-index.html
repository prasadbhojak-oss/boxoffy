How the Hype Index Works

The Hype Score (0–100) blends four inputs: forecast tier (40% weight), pre-release signal strength (30%), proximity to release (20%), and BCM forecast confidence (10%). A score of 70+ flags HIGH HYPE; 50-69 is ELEVATED; 35-49 is MODERATE; below 35 is LOW HYPE. The score reflects pre-release anticipation, not a guaranteed outcome.

Boxoffy Confidence Model (BCM) v4.0

Each film routes to one of five segments — mass-tentpole, mass-multiplex, multiplex-genre, indie-art, south-mass — plus a Hollywood-India special module. Each segment uses its own historical comparable pool (82 films total) and segment-specific genome distance weights. Pre-release signals (awareness, engagement, intent) adjust the prior. Outputs include a Day 1 point forecast and a 90% credible interval.

Confidence Tiers and Hard Caps

BCM never shows High Confidence when the film is more than 30 days from release. At 60+ days, confidence is hard-capped at Low. If the comparable pool has fewer than 5 films or segment volatility (CV) exceeds 0.90, additional caps apply. This is a deliberate guardrail: studios should never receive a confidently wrong forecast simply because the math produced one.

Outlier Flags

When pre-release signal patterns move outside 1.5–3 standard deviations from segment baseline, BCM flags an anomaly. These flags are directional — the model's confidence interval already accounts for normal statistical variance, but anomaly periods (Dhurandhar, Saiyaara, Pushpa-class events) historically produce 1.5–2× upside outside the band. Outlier flags exist to invite manual editorial review, not to predict the magnitude of the deviation.

Known Limitations

BCM v4.0 uses hard segment routing — hybrid films (Sam Bahadur, Brahmastra-style) may route imperfectly. Soft routing planned for v4.1. ATB-class outliers occur 1-2 times per year; the model does not chase them precision-wise because forcing such precision overfits the system. Where signals genuinely warrant suspicion, you'll see an OUTLIER EVENT IN PROGRESS flag and the recommendation to defer high-confidence calls.

What is the Boxoffy Hype Index?

The Boxoffy Hype Index is a 0-100 score that measures pre-release anticipation for upcoming Indian films. It combines the Boxoffy Confidence Model (BCM) forecast tier, signal strength (awareness, engagement, intent), proximity to release, and confidence calibration.

How is BCM different from other box office forecasts?

BCM is the only Indian box office model with: log-rupee Bayesian regression, segment-routed comparable pools, transparent confidence intervals, hard caps preventing overclaiming, and explicit failure case studies. Unlike trade analyst predictions, BCM publishes its methodology, comparable choices, and historical accuracy.

Why are some forecasts shown with Low confidence?

Confidence reflects how trustworthy the forecast is — not how exciting the film is. Films more than 60 days from release are automatically capped at Low because pre-release signals can move 30%+ in the final week. A Low confidence forecast is honest signal, not weak signal.

How accurate has the model been?

On a 23-film historical backtest, BCM v4.0 achieves 87% in-band accuracy (forecast 90% interval contains actual D1) with a Mean Absolute Error of ₹8.6 Cr. On the multiplex-genre segment specifically (the cleanest pool), it hits 88% accuracy. Performance varies by segment — see the methodology page for full backtest disclosure.
