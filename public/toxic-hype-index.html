Boxoffy Hype Index · Investigation · Toxic · Aug 26, 2026

Toxic wants to be India's next global crossover.

Fandango is already cutting the full trailer for American audiences. The English-language version is in the can. The festive worldwide date is locked. Everything is moving except the one announcement that matters most — and three months later, Ramayana shows Yash exactly what a finished global deal looks like.

The tell is in the last three rows. A film gets a Fandango trailer push, an English-language cut and a worldwide date before it names an international distributor only when the marketing is deliberately running ahead of the deal — building demand to strengthen the negotiation. Toxic was already delayed twice, from March to June to August, explicitly to lock global distribution. That context makes the empty distributor chair the single most important fact about this release.

The ambition is explicit, and it's the right one. Shot in English, pitched to international buyers at CinemaCon, a period crime saga with global-festival DNA in director Geetu Mohandas — this is a film reaching for the RRR lane, where an Indian title crosses into genuine Western viewership rather than diaspora-only. But RRR crossed over on two things Toxic does not yet have: a confirmed wide US release through a committed distributor, and a months-long awards campaign that kept it in the Western conversation. Toxic has the pitch and the Fandango trailer buzz. It does not, today, have the distribution or the festival runway. Verdict: positioned to try, not yet equipped. The next four weeks — trailer, and crucially a distributor announcement — decide which film this becomes.

No Hollywood studio, no named overseas partner for the English cut. Trade reporting is explicit that Warner, Lionsgate and A24 are fan speculation. The film moved twice — March to June to August — to align exactly this. Until it's announced, the width of the US release is unknowable, and that's the difference between a diaspora run and a real crossover.

This is the investigative core. A Fandango trailer proves promotional intent in the US; it does not prove theatrical distribution. Fandango cuts trailers for plenty of films whose release width is still being negotiated. So the honest read is a marketing machine deliberately running ahead of an unfinished deal — which is a strong position if the deal lands in August, and an exposed one if it doesn't.

Why the ranges are wide and honest. KGF 2 is the demand proof — it opened enormous and finished around ₹1,250 Cr worldwide. But Toxic is a period gangster drama, not a mass-action sequel with a pre-sold hero arc, and Yash has been off screen for four years. The base case assumes the KGF audience shows up strongly for the star; the bull case assumes the crossover pitch and female-led marketing genuinely widen the room. The overseas line is deliberately cautious: without a named distributor and a known screen count, a big Western number is a hope, not a projection. The Fandango push nudges the ceiling, not the floor.

How Toxic feeds into Ramayana.

ToxicAUGUST · the auditionYash leads · crossover test
RamayanaNOVEMBER · the arenaYash as Ravana · Sony global

Here is the thread that makes this more than one film's release. Yash has two global swings in one autumn. In August he leads Toxic — his own production, his crossover thesis, his distributor gap to solve. In November he plays Ravana in Ramayana, where Sony Pictures has already locked the international distribution he's still chasing for Toxic. August is the audition; November is the arena. If Toxic travels, Yash walks into Ramayana as a proven global draw and Ravana becomes a worldwide event villain. If Toxic stays a domestic hit, Ramayana becomes the vehicle that does his crossing-over for him, on Sony's rails instead of his own. Either way, the KGF star spends the back half of 2026 testing the same question twice — can an Indian star open a movie for the world — with two very different sets of distribution muscle behind him. That's the story worth watching, and Toxic's distributor announcement is the first shoe to drop.
