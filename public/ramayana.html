The IMAX squeeze

4 Nov 154 screens · 11.17%

Ramayana's IMAX run is one night. Across these three nights, 81% of every IMAX ticket sold is for 4 November, and only 26 cinemas keep the IMAX hall on all three dates. Godzilla Minus Zero takes IMAX on 3 November — 190 halls, every one at 7:00p local. Ramayana gets about 150 halls the next night, and 100 of those are the same room. By 6 November, when Godzilla opens wide, 80 of those 100 no longer carry Ramayana in IMAX at all.

Screen counts are listed showtimes, which do not depend on a seat map reading. Godzilla's hall list is from our 4 October scan; its own ticket counts are not shown because too many of its seat maps failed to read.

Method

How this is counted

Every showtime on sale is read directly from the seat map — total seats and seats still free. Tickets sold is the difference. The ticket count is measured; only the ticket price is assumed.

Duplicate listings for the same room at the same time are merged, keeping the largest seat map, so premium sub-allocations are not double counted.

Canada is two chains counted two different ways. Cineplex publishes how many seats are free but never how big the room is, so the room size is worked out backwards — the largest availability a room ever shows is a floor on its capacity — and what we publish is a deliberate minimum: 5,555 tickets across 27 cinemas and 33 auditoriums, 5.61% of 99,051 seats, over the seven dates Cineplex has on sale. One of those auditoriums appears in a single showtime and reads zero by construction. Cineplex does not mark seats the cinema holds back, so a few house seats sit inside that figure. Landmark is counted, and about a third of it may be held seats. Landmark's robots.txt answers 403, so no scanner of ours has ever fetched it; every Landmark showtime is opened by hand in a browser instead. Its map draws every seat but has one off-state, so 1,952 seats gone is what we can say, not 1,952 sales. It is two kinds of room now and they are not equally well understood. Six IMAX rooms, twelve showtimes, read three times over — the same twelve each time: 650, then 676, then 769. All six cinemas moved on the last read, and a block does not move, so that +93 is buying. Those rooms also repeat the same floor on both nights — Waterloo 91 of its 223, Whitby 82 of 344 — and that floor is carried by each showtime, so 590 of the 769 is held inventory and the clean read is 179. Each room has only two sittings, which is below the three our own test needs, so 179 is a hard floor and 769 a ceiling. Three ScreenX rooms, thirty-six showtimes, 554 seats gone — and never opened before this read. None of that is buying; it is inventory we had never looked at. It is also the first part of this chain we could test properly: two of those rooms run fourteen showtimes each, where the IMAX rooms run two. The per-showtime figures that test needs are not in hand yet, so ScreenX is untested for blocks and the clean floor for the chain stays 179 rather than rising with it. We publish the 1,323 on the same seats-gone basis the UK uses and state the split here. Nine rooms at eight cinemas is a fraction of that chain, and it plays in more than these two formats, so Canada can still only move up. Those twelve showtimes have now been opened three times, and the matched-pair method they proved has since been used on every market on this page. Nothing was added to the worklist, so every ticket of movement there is movement rather than coverage. The second read, nine and a half hours after the first, added +26 — Whitby +17, Kanata +4, Waterloo +4, Calgary +1, Kingston and Nanaimo flat. The third, fourteen and a half hours after that, added +93, and this time every one of the six moved — Waterloo +40, Whitby +22, Calgary +14, Kanata +10, Kingston +4, Nanaimo +3. A block is a count that does not move, so all 119 belong to the clean read, which is why it goes 60 → 86 → 179 while the block stays at 590.

Australia is the most completely counted market on this page, and New Zealand now sits beside it. Both Australian chains are read end to end. Event Cinemas publishes seats still free, so the Canadian method transfers: 2,061 tickets over all 425 shows at 38 cinemas, 2.07% of 99,604 seats. HOYTS is read a better way — its booking system publishes the whole seat plan per session and marks sold seats separately from seats the cinema is holding back, so we count only the sold ones: 6,911 tickets across all 434 shows at 47 cinemas, 9.63% of 71,775 seats, with 2,501 held seats left out. Australia totals 8,972. New Zealand adds 429 across 14 Event cinemas, and that one is Event only — HOYTS New Zealand and the independents are not counted. An earlier Australian figure of 1,486 was wrong and the correction is ours. Until 18 Oct we believed HOYTS could not be counted and published a hand-read sample of 13 shows at 4 of its 47 cinemas, extended by a model with a ±45% band. The model said 819. The count said 3,060. The error was not a small one and it was in our method, not in the market. Prices here are part observed, part assumed: IMAX A$45, ScreenX A$36.50 and Xtremescreen A$27 were read off HOYTS' own ticket page and cover 82% of Australian tickets; the remaining formats, and all of Event's, carry those equivalents. New Zealand's price is theirs — Event NZ prints one on every session.

The Netherlands is the cleanest foreign read on this page. 124 showtimes at 31 cinemas, 100 of them read by hand in a browser because neither Pathé nor Vue can be fetched by a scanner of ours: 2,639 seats gone, 9.9% of the 24,114 seats read. 88 showtimes were read on both 22 and 24 October and went 1,869 → 2,113 — 244 tickets of real buying, and for the second read running not one showtime fell. Vue Kerkrade, Enschede and others were read for the first time (+273); eleven showtimes were pulled (−53). Pathé Ypenburg in Den Haag is 195 of 213 seats gone for Thursday 7pm. Seven Pathé Dolby showtimes now say "volgeboekt" (fully booked) with no seat map, some of them a day after selling a single seat — that is a room taken off sale, not a room filled, so they keep last night's 253 as a floor rather than being counted full. Our block test flags 282 seats in four rooms — mostly Vue Kerkrade, which reads 77 to 85 on every showing, the flat shape of a held block — so real bookings are 2,357 to 2,639. Every Pathé room decays properly (682, 780, 618, 328, 78, 94, 59 by date), which is what buying looks like and what a block never does. Neither chain marks held seats separately, so this is seats gone rather than confirmed sales, the same basis the UK headline uses. The price here is ours, unlike Germany's and India's: Vue prints €15.00 at Hilversum and €13.00 at Eindhoven and LAB-1 prints €14.00, but Pathé — 2,317 of the 2,639 — shows nothing before seat selection, so €14.00 is an assumption applied to a counted ticket. Nine Pathé cinemas returned no showtimes, but their date strip only offered 10 November onward, so that is a gap in our reach and not a confirmed zero. Kinepolis lists the film for 11 November with nothing yet on sale. Sweden is still a measured zero. It is now listed at 25 cinemas, 31 showtimes. Seven seat maps at five independents were read and every one says all seats free — 230 of 230, 82 of 82. It is published as a zero rather than left off the page, because a reader who sees no row assumes we did not look. It is also not Sweden: Filmstaden, 17 of those cinemas and the country's biggest chain, blocked every read, so what is on this page is five independents rather than a country.

What this page is

And what the numbers do and do not mean

This is a live count of advance bookings for Ramayana: Rise of a Legend, taken straight off the cinemas' own booking pages. It is not a prediction and it is not a trade estimate. For every single showtime that is on sale, we open the seat map, count the seats in the room and count the seats still free. The difference is what has gone. Then we do it again a few hours later.

That is the whole method, and it is the reason the number can be trusted: nobody is guessing. Where we cannot count something, we say so on the page instead of filling the gap with a model — and when a model of ours has turned out wrong, we have said that too, in the Method panel above, with the old figure left visible.

The one thing that is assumed

Ticket prices. No cinema chain anywhere publishes what it actually charged for a seat, so every money figure on this page is our own price applied to a counted ticket. We print the prices we used. If you only trust one column, trust the ticket count.

Why each country reads differently

Because the chains publish different things. HOYTS in Australia shows the full seat plan and marks which seats are sold and which the cinema is holding back, so a sold seat there really is a sale. Cineplex in Canada and Event in Australia publish only the seats still free, so we recover the room size from the busiest and emptiest shows and the result is a floor — it can only move up. Cineworld in the UK will not say whether a seat off sale was bought or blocked, so the UK figure is labelled seats off sale, not tickets sold.

Mixing those into one worldwide occupancy percentage would be meaningless, so we do not publish one. Tickets add up across markets. Percentages do not.

Where the film is on sale

The United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and Germany are counted seat by seat. Germany is the one market where the ticket price is sometimes the cinema's own rather than our assumption — German cinemas print it on the booking page, and about half our German tickets carry a price read off one. The other half use our own figure, the same way every other market does. India is covered separately in our Ramayana box office preview.

Germany also has something no other market here does: a dubbed version competing with the original. The subtitled original is taking about five tickets for every one the German dub sells, seat for seat.

Common questions

Answered plainly

How many Ramayana tickets have been sold so far?

The counter at the top of this page is the live figure. It is not an estimate. Every showtime on sale is opened and its seat map read, seat by seat, so the ticket number is counted rather than modelled. It is rescanned several times a day and the page shows the time of the last scan.

When does Ramayana: Rise of a Legend release?

Opening day is 5 November 2026 in North America, the United Kingdom and Australia. America also has preview shows the night before, on 4 November. The UK and Australia open cold, with no previews at all.

How does Boxoffy count presales?

One seat map at a time. For every showtime we read the total seats in the room and the seats still free, and the difference is what has gone. Duplicate listings of the same room at the same time are merged so a premium block is not counted twice. The ticket count is measured. Only the ticket price is assumed.

Are the dollar figures real box office collections?

No. No cinema chain publishes what it charged. The money figures are our own ticket price applied to a counted ticket, and we print the price we used. Treat the ticket count as the hard number and the money as an indication.

Why does the UK figure say seats off sale instead of tickets sold?

Because the chain does not say which is which. A seat the booking page will not sell you may be a seat somebody bought, or a seat the cinema is holding back for wheelchair users, companions or late sales. Most of the UK figure never changes across a room's whole run, which is the signature of a block rather than a sale, so we publish a midpoint and show the raw number beside it.

Which countries are counted?

The United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and Germany are counted seat by seat. Germany is the only market where any cinema publishes its own ticket price, and about half its tickets carry one; the rest use our figure, the same as every other market. India is not presale-tracked on this page.

Is Ramayana sold out anywhere?

Sold-out shows are flagged on the page as they happen. Premiere night in America and the biggest HOYTS rooms in Sydney have been the fullest so far. Most showtimes anywhere in the world have sold nothing at all yet, which is normal this far from release.

How often does this page update?

The page re-reads our published scan file every two minutes while it is open, and the scan itself runs several times a day. The Check for update button re-reads that same file; it does not make the data newer.
