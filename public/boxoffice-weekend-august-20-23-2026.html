Day 6 alone: ₹1.40 Cr across 6,475 shows at roughly 8% occupancy. Day series: 5.75 · 13.50 · 7.25 · 2.00 · 2.25 · 1.40. Three-day opening of ₹26.50 Cr made it the third-biggest Sunny Deol opener ever — and then the floor fell out.

Most of the coverage has picked the wrong comparison. "Emraan beat Sunny" is true and mostly uninteresting. Both films opened into the same holiday, on the same screens, in the same week. What matters is the shape of each film's own curve, and only one of them has a healthy one.

Why Batwara 1947 didn't work.

August 15, 2026 fell on a Saturday. That is the most important fact about this release and the one almost no coverage has priced in.

A Partition drama opening the Friday before an Independence Day Saturday isn't bought the way films are normally bought. The audience doesn't have to want this film. It has to want a film, that day. Our advance work flagged this case before release: when Aug 15 lands on a weekend, a patriotic title's event day shifts to Day 2 and the advance underindexes it. The model handles the opening fine. What it can't tell you is whether the spike converts into anything.

The arithmetic

A Hindi release with ordinary word-of-mouth moves Friday to Saturday at roughly 1.35–1.50×. Take the midpoint, 1.42×, as the counterfactual — Batwara's Saturday with no holiday attached.

Three tells, in order of severity

One: Sunday failed a normal Sunday test. Day 3 came in at ₹7.25 Cr — 1.26× the Friday, against 1.55–1.70× for a healthy Hindi release. Batwara's first non-holiday day underperformed a standard Sunday off its own opening. The curve was already negative before the working week began.

Two: Monday dropped 72%. Sunday-to-Monday for a functioning film is −55% to −65%. This sits outside the band entirely.

Three: no weekday base formed. Days 4–6 delivered ₹5.65 Cr — ₹1.88 Cr a day against a ₹5.75 Cr opening, a ratio of 0.33 where legs hold 0.45–0.60. Awarapan 2 runs 0.39 on a far larger base, and its Tuesday came in above its Monday.

The genre point

Here is the uncomfortable version: the Partition drama has never had a commercial baseline in Hindi cinema. Gadar gets cited as proof it does. Gadar was a spike driven by Sunny Deol's screen persona at one very specific moment, and nothing around it before or since has established a floor.

Quality isn't the missing variable either. Main Vaapas Aaunga carried an 8.1 IMDb score and still finished below break-even. When a genre's best-reviewed films underperform alongside its worst, execution has stopped being the explanation. The audience for the subject is just smaller than the industry keeps assuming, and it gets mistaken for the audience for a star. Batwara 1947 had Rahman, Santosh Sivan, Santoshi, Aamir Khan Productions and Sunny Deol. When a package that strong can't hold a Monday, the problem sits upstream of the film.

Our own ledger. We called Batwara 1947's Day 1 at ₹5.5 Cr against a 4.0–7.5 band. It landed at ₹5.75 Cr — inside 5%, our most accurate India opening call of this cycle. The comparables held the line against an in-house push toward ₹9–10 Cr. What we did not publish in advance was a hold call, and that is the number that actually mattered here. Noted for the ledger.

Where we've widened, and why. Our last two India calls both missed low: Awarapan 2's opening by 30%, Spider-Man's second weekend by 35–40%. The US weekend: Spider-Man goes for four.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day is chasing a fourth consecutive number one. It led last weekend with $70.7M across 4,539 sites — a 51% hold — crossed $803.9M domestic on Tuesday as the fastest film ever to $800M, passed $2B worldwide, and overtook No Way Home to become the highest-grossing Spider-Man ever. Where we differ. The consensus floor on Spider-Man is $30M — a 58% drop. Nothing in four weeks supports that; its legs are the whole story of the run, and its Week 3 hold was 51% against a far tougher comparison. We put the band at 45–52% off, or $34–39M, and call $36M. On Insidious, the franchise ladder reads $40.27M (Chapter 2), $33M (Red Door), $29.58M (Last Key), $22.69M (Chapter 3). A sixth entry three years on, carrying 55% on Rotten Tomatoes, lands nearer the bottom of that ladder than the middle: $24M, under the street midpoint.

Third place is where the consensus contradicts itself. The street puts The Odyssey third and leaves PAW Patrol outside the top three. But PAW Patrol carries 10% of national showtime share against The Odyssey's 6%, and opened last weekend at $20.5M against $23.2M. A film with nearly double the showtimes of the one ranked above it doesn't usually finish behind it. We have them at $13.5M and $13M, which is a coin toss, and we'd rather say so than invent a gap. The variable is back-to-school: it has started in enough of the country to start cutting family matinees.

The Odyssey is the quiet achievement of the summer: $508.78M domestic and $1.316B worldwide in six weeks, now tracking toward Barbie's $1.447B. Three hours of Homer, and it is out-legging most of the tentpoles it opened against.

🎵 The music desk
Toxic · three composers, one problem
The album splits across Ravi Basrur, Vishal Mishra and Tanishk Bagchi: a Kannada mass composer, a Hindi melody specialist and a Bollywood chart mechanic, all on one soundtrack. Nobody assembles that lineup for musical reasons. It tells you how hard the film is chasing the Hindi and Telugu tracks alongside its home market.
Batwara 1947 · Rahman, unrewarded
A.R. Rahman with Javed Akhtar on lyrics is the film's strongest creative asset, and its reception has been the warmest thing about the release. It has moved no tickets since Sunday. A soundtrack amplifies demand; it doesn't create it. It can raise the ceiling on an audience that already exists and can do nothing about one that doesn't.

The through-line. Awarapan 2's jukebox worked because the 2007 soundtrack had spent eighteen years building an audience the sequel could walk in and collect. Batwara had no such deposit to draw on. Music converts when it arrives second.

On the horizon.

Next Wednesday is the whole story. Toxic opens Aug 26 into a board where Awarapan 2 is a fortnight old and everything else is in tail. It takes screens from Batwara 1947 first, which effectively ends that run somewhere around ₹45–50 Cr net India, then from Khalifa and Irumudi.

Beyond that the road bends to Diwali and Ramayana: Part One on Nov 6. Standing note holds: Nov 6 is Dhanteras, Nov 8 the softest evening of the festival, so the three-day will understate it. Judge Ramayana on five days to Bhai Dooj.
